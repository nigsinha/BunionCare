Important

Comfort first. No forcing.

These exercises may improve foot strength, mobility and comfort, but they cannot guarantee that an established bunion will be straightened or permanently corrected.

  • Stop if an exercise causes or increases pain.
  • Do not force the big toe into position.
  • If symptoms are severe, worsening, or affecting walking, seek professional advice.
Good to know

Common questions

Can exercises permanently remove a bunion? +

Exercises generally do not reverse an established bony deformity. They are better viewed as a way to work on mobility, strength and symptom management.

Should I use a toe separator? +

A comfortable separator may reduce rubbing or pressure for some people. It should not be tight or painful, and it is not a guaranteed correction device.

When should I get professional help? +

Consider an assessment if pain is significant or persistent, the deformity is progressing, walking becomes difficult, or footwear is increasingly uncomfortable.

How often should I exercise? +

Follow the dose shown for each exercise and prioritize comfortable, controlled movement over doing more repetitions.
